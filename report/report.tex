\hypertarget{model}{%
\section{1. Model}\label{model}}

\textbf{Problem (brief §2).} Customers \(i=1..N\) with demands \(d_i\),
\(K\) identical vehicles of capacity \(Q\), Euclidean costs.
Cluster-first / route-second: the quantum part decides \emph{which}
customers share a vehicle; each cluster is then routed exactly
(Held--Karp). Decision variables \(x_{ik}\in\{0,1\}\) (customer \(i\) on
vehicle \(k\)), one-hot per customer, \(n = NK\) atoms: \textbf{8 / 30 /
60} on S / M / L, no slack. Every derivation is in
\texttt{docs/DERIVATIONS.md} (§1--§4) and \texttt{docs/MODEL.md}; this
report states results and decisions.

\textbf{Objective.} Total pairwise distance inside each cluster,
\(f=\sum_k\sum_{i<j} d_{ij}x_{ik}x_{jk}\) --- additive over clusters, so
the exact set-partition DP (\texttt{cvrp/exact.py}) certifies the
\emph{model} optimum on every tier.

\textbf{Constraints and what the device sees (DERIVATIONS §2--§4).} At
\(\Omega=0\), Eq. (1) assigns a bitstring the cost
\(E(x) = -\delta_f\sum_i x_i + \sum_{i<j} C_6 r_{ij}^{-6} x_i x_j\): one
\emph{uniform} linear coefficient (laser) and \emph{positive} couplings
(positions). Exactly-one {[}={]}: squared penalty
\(A\sum_i(1-\sum_k x_{ik})^2\) → \(h=-A\) on every variable (uniform
(OK)), \(J=2A\) on each customer's \(K\)-clique (positive (OK)).
Capacity {[}≤{]}: unbalanced penalization
\(\sum_k[-\lambda_1(Q-L_k)+\lambda_2(Q-L_k)^2]\) (Montañez-Barrera et
al.~2024), expanded; its per-customer linear part \(c_i\sum_k x_{ik}\)
is a \emph{constant} on one-hot strings (the one-hot identity), so it is
dropped --- \(\lambda_1\) disappears and what remains is the coupling
\(+2\lambda_2 d_id_j\) on same-vehicle pairs, i.e.~a
\textbf{load-balance term} \(\lambda_2\sum_k L_k^2\). It is soft: for
\(K=2\) the balance optimum is capacity-feasible whenever a feasible
split exists, for \(K\ge3\) only likely, so we score feasible decodes
only and report the feasibility rate (Pitfall 9.1). Weights:
\(A=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}(d_{ij}+2\lambda_2 d_id_j)+1\) (provable: every
argmin is one-hot), \(\lambda_2=0.15\,R/(2Q)\) from a 40-seed
brute-force scan. Brute-force checker on tier S: argmin feasible and
optimal on 5/5 shipped instances; \(A\) survives one halving and fails
at ¼; \(\lambda_2\) is not binding on these instances
(\texttt{out/gate1/}).

\hypertarget{embedding-derivations-5}{%
\section{3. Embedding (DERIVATIONS §5)}\label{embedding-derivations-5}}

Fidelity formula reported everywhere: \(\|U/\alpha - J\|_F/\|J\|_F\)
over off-diagonals, with the worst-pair error next to it. The generic
least-squares embedder cannot realise the one-hot rook's graph: the
\(K\) copies of a customer sit 5 µm apart, so the ``zero'' cross-vehicle
couplings come out as large as the wanted ones (rel. Frobenius 0.95; on
S\_seed04 the register's own ground state was infeasible). We therefore
ask for less: on one-hot strings each customer pair contributes exactly
one coupling, so only \emph{same-vehicle minus cross-vehicle} must equal
\(\alpha w_{ij}\) (identity 5.7b). Each customer becomes a rigid 5 µm
\(K\)-gon (clique pinned at the minimum separation,
\(\alpha = C_6/5^6/2A\)); centres and orientations are fitted so the
device energies of all one-hot strings match \(\alpha E_\text{QUBO}\),
with explicit single-flip defect gaps and a fitted \(\delta_f\) (a
constant on the subspace). Registers are cached and committed
(\texttt{embeddings/}). Diagnostics per run: full-matrix fidelity,
subspace fidelity, and the realised-register check (device argmin
feasible? equal to the model optimum? defect gap). On tier S: argmin =
model optimum on 5/5 instances.

\includegraphics[width=0.54\textwidth,height=\textheight]{../report/figures/couplings.png}

\textbf{Where it breaks (tier M, \(K=3\)).} Triangles 5 µm on a side
cannot be far from each other's neighbours: the subspace-fit residual is
38 (S: 0.15--0.32), defects fall below their parents and the model
optimum ranks \textasciitilde10 000th among one-hot strings; opening the
polygons lowers the energy scale without fixing the ordering
(DERIVATIONS §5.7, STATUS). This is the brief's §4 warning in its
sharpest form, and it is a property of one-hot encodings on a 2-D
\(1/r^6\) register.

\hypertarget{pulse-derivations-6}{%
\section{4. Pulse (DERIVATIONS §6)}\label{pulse-derivations-6}}

Three-point Ω ramp (15 \% / 70 \% / 15 \%) and a linear δ sweep from
\(\delta_0=-\min(\max(|\delta_f|,2\Omega_{\max}),\delta_{\max})\) to
\(\delta_f\) while Ω is on its plateau; every sequence is built with
Pulser on \texttt{AnalogDevice} and validated
(\texttt{to\_abstract\_repr()}, limits read from the device object).
Scan on three tier-S instances, scored by P(reach), best gap and
feasibility (never mean cost): longer is monotonically better;
\(\Omega=0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\) beats full and half power;
\(\delta_f\times\{0.8,1,1.25\}\) peaks at the fitted value.
\textbf{Chosen: 5800 ns, \(0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\), fitted \(\delta_f\)}
(35--38 rad/µs on S, inside ±125.66). Emulators: pulser-simulation
(qutip) for every headline row, emu-sv as a cross-check, and an in-house
exact state-vector integrator built from Pulser's own samples for scans
(agrees with qutip to sampling noise, TV ≈ 0.05 at 500 shots); emu-mps
for M/L with the bond dimension recorded.

Reading: with the structure-aware register and the scanned pulse, the
device returns the model-optimal clustering in 89--99 \% of raw shots on
all five tier-S instances (uniform random bitstrings: 0.7--1 \%; generic
embedder: 3--13 \%). \textbf{Useful quantum work (brief §9.2):} the fair
control is an \emph{assignment-valid} random sampler --- uniform random
assignments encoded one-hot (\texttt{random-onehot}), which reaches the
model optimum with P = 2/16 ≈ 0.12 on every tier-S instance (and is
capacity-feasible 25--63 \% of the time); the headline quantum rows
reach it with P = 0.94 / 0.95 / 0.89 on S\_seed00/01/04, a 7--8× factor
at equal decoding, so the register --- not the decoder --- does the
work. \textbf{Ablation --- the exactly-one margin is physically
load-bearing.} \texttt{onehot-ub-tight} replaces the provable rule
\(A=\max_i\sum_j w_{ij}+1\) by the tight bound \(A = R_w/K\,(1+1\%)\)
(still sound: the optimum stays the QUBO ground state, checker-verified)
and changes nothing else. With the same embedder and the pulse tuned for
the original \(A\), P(reach optimum) goes 0.94 → 0.03 (S\_seed00; the
realised register's ground state becomes the third-best clustering),
0.95 → 0.56 (S\_seed01) and 0.89 → 0.96 (S\_seed04): a smaller \(A\)
shrinks the single-flip defect gaps the sweep must resolve and leaves
less margin for the embedding error. The headline stays
\texttt{onehot-ub}; the tight rows are in results.csv as the ablation
(qutip: 0.03 / 0.52 / 0.94; the in-house emulator agrees within sampling
noise). Where P(reach \(C^*\)) is low with a 0 \% model gap
(S\_seed03/04) the loss is the decomposition, not the device.
qubo-solver's own pipeline (BLaDE + its drive shaper, 5940 ns) reaches
0.18--0.79 of the model optimum on four instances and fails on S\_seed04
(27 \% model gap); its classical tabu search finds every model optimum,
and its default SA path does not (a single-start greedy descent at
\(T_0=5\) --- we report a stated-parameter SA instead).

\hypertarget{what-did-not-work}{%
\section{6. What did not work}\label{what-did-not-work}}

\texttt{docs/DID\_NOT\_WORK.md}, one sentence each: no PyPI from the
sandboxes; CPLEX not installable; a typed number caught by review;
qubo-solver's default SA; a blocking calibration job; non-reproducible
embedding optimiser (fixed by committing registers); the tight-\(A\)
ablation; and the one-hot rook's graph not embedding for \(K=3\) --- the
honest limit of this encoding on this device.
