\hypertarget{model}{%
\section{1. Model}\label{model}}

\textbf{What we chose, why, and what it costs.} Cluster-first /
route-second: the register decides \emph{which} customers share a
vehicle; each cluster is then routed exactly (Held--Karp). The full
(customer, vehicle, position) encoding needs \(\sim N^2K\) variables ---
32 / 300 / 900 on S / M / L --- against 80 atoms (DERIVATIONS Q1);
clustering needs \(NK\). The price is a \emph{decomposition gap} (our
objective's best clustering is not always the CVRP optimum), reported in
§5.

\textbf{Variables and objective (brief §2).} Customers \(i=1..N\) with
demands \(d_i\), \(K\) identical vehicles of capacity \(Q\), Euclidean
costs \(d_{ij}\). One-hot \(x_{ik}\in\{0,1\}\) (customer \(i\) on
vehicle \(k\)), \(n=NK\) atoms: \textbf{8 / 30 / 60} on S / M / L, no
slack. Objective: total pairwise distance inside each cluster,
\(f=\sum_k\sum_{i<j} d_{ij}x_{ik}x_{jk}\), additive over clusters, so an
exact set-partition DP (\texttt{cvrp/exact.py}) certifies the
\emph{model} optimum on every tier.

\textbf{Constraints → penalties (DERIVATIONS §4,
\texttt{docs/MODEL.md}).} Exactly-one {[}={]}: squared penalty
\(A\sum_i(1-\sum_k x_{ik})^2\) → \(h=-A\) on every variable, \(J=2A\) on
each customer's \(K\)-clique. Capacity {[}≤{]}: unbalanced penalization
\(\sum_k[-\lambda_1(Q-L_k)+\lambda_2(Q-L_k)^2]\) (Montañez-Barrera et
al.~2024), expanded. Its per-customer linear part \(c_i\sum_k x_{ik}\)
is a \emph{constant} on one-hot strings (the one-hot identity), so it is
dropped: \(\lambda_1\) disappears and what remains is
\(+2\lambda_2 d_id_j\) on same-vehicle pairs, a \textbf{soft
load-balance term} \(\lambda_2\sum_k L_k^2\). For \(K=2\) its optimum is
capacity-feasible whenever a feasible split exists; for \(K\ge3\) only
likely --- so we score feasible decodes only and report the feasibility
rate (Pitfall 9.1).

\textbf{What the device sees.} At \(\Omega=0\) the final Hamiltonian is
a QUBO with one \emph{uniform} linear coefficient (\(\delta_f\)) and
\emph{positive} couplings (\(C_6/r^6\)) --- exactly the pattern above:
every \(J>0\), every \(h=-A\). Weights:
\(A=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}(d_{ij}+2\lambda_2 d_id_j)+1\) (provable: every
argmin is one-hot), \(\lambda_2=0.15\,R/(2Q)\) from a 40-seed
brute-force scan. Checker on tier S (\texttt{out/gate1/}): argmin
feasible and optimal on 5/5 shipped instances; \(A\) survives one
halving and fails at ¼; \(\lambda_2\) is not binding on these instances.

\hypertarget{embedding-derivations-5}{%
\section{3. Embedding (DERIVATIONS §5)}\label{embedding-derivations-5}}

\textbf{What we chose, why, and what it costs.} A generic least-squares
placement cannot realise the one-hot rook's graph, so we fit the
register on the one-hot subspace only (§5.7). For \(K=2\) (tier S) the
fit realises the model; for \(K=3\) (tier M) the constraints embed but
the objective does not (§5, DERIVATIONS §6.7).

\textbf{Why the generic embedder fails.} The \(K\) copies of a customer
form a \(2A\)-clique 5 µm apart, so a neighbouring customer's atom is at
nearly the same distance from all \(K\) copies: the ``zero''
cross-vehicle couplings come out as large as the wanted ones. Measured:
rel. Frobenius error 0.95 (\(\|U/\alpha-J\|_F/\|J\|_F\) over
off-diagonals, always reported with the worst-pair error) and, on
S\_seed04, an infeasible device ground state (model optimum at rank 9).

\textbf{Ask for less.} On one-hot strings each customer pair contributes
exactly one coupling, so only \emph{same-vehicle minus cross-vehicle}
must equal \(\alpha w_{ij}\) (identity 5.7b); cross couplings need only
be constant. Each customer becomes a rigid 5 µm \(K\)-gon (clique at the
minimum separation, \(\alpha=C_6/5^6/2A\)); centres and orientations are
fitted (L-BFGS, 6 seeded starts) so the device energies of all one-hot
strings match \(\alpha E_\text{QUBO}\), with explicit single-flip defect
gaps and a fitted \(\delta_f\) (a constant on the subspace: it only sets
the defect gaps). Registers are cached and committed
(\texttt{embeddings/}). Per run: full-matrix and subspace fidelity, and
the \emph{realised-register check} (device argmin feasible and optimal?
smallest defect gap). Tier S: 5/5 yes.

\textbf{Where it breaks (tier M, \(K=3\)).} Triangles 5 µm on a side
cannot be far from each other's neighbours: the subspace-fit residual is
38 (S: 0.06--0.32) and the model optimum ranks \textasciitilde10 000th
of 59 049 one-hot strings; opening the triangles to 6--10 µm does not
fix the ordering (rank ≥ 1 279; DERIVATIONS §5.7) --- the brief's §4
warning in its sharpest form, a property of one-hot encodings on a 2-D
\(1/r^6\) register. The cliques themselves do embed, under a bound found
on M (§6.7): at \(\Omega=0\) a \(K\)-gon's one-hot state is its ground
state only if \(0<\delta_f<U_\text{clique}=55.1\) rad/µs. S fits land at
0.49--0.68 \(U\); the ill-conditioned M fit ran to the device maximum
125.66 (2.28 \(U\)), so the embedder now enforces the bound (fallback
0.65 \(U\)).

\hypertarget{pulse-derivations-6}{%
\section{4. Pulse (DERIVATIONS §6)}\label{pulse-derivations-6}}

\textbf{Chosen: \(T=5800\) ns (device maximum minus 200 ns),
\(\Omega=0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\), fitted \(\delta_f\)} (35--38 rad/µs on
S, inside ±125.66). Shape: three-point Ω ramp (15 \% / 70 \% / 15 \% of
\(T\)), linear δ sweep from
\(\delta_0=-\min(\max(|\delta_f|,2\Omega_{\max}),\delta_{\max})\) to
\(\delta_f\) while Ω is on its plateau; every sequence is built with
Pulser on \texttt{AnalogDevice} and validated
(\texttt{to\_abstract\_repr()}, limits from the device object). Why: a
scan on three tier-S instances (Fig. 1 right, §6.6), scored by P(reach),
best gap and feasibility, never mean cost: longer is monotonically
better (2000 → 4000 → 5800 ns at every Ω); \(0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\) beats
full and half power at 4000 and 5800 ns;
\(\delta_f\times\{0.8,1,1.25\}\) peaks at the fitted value.
S\_seed02/03, not used in the scan, reach the model optimum in 99 \% /
96 \% of shots with these settings. Emulators: pulser-simulation (qutip)
for every S headline row; emu-sv cross-check (S\_seed00: 0.96 vs 0.94);
an in-house exact state-vector integrator from Pulser's own samples for
the scans (TV 0.05--0.07 from qutip at 500 shots = sampling noise);
emu-mps for M/L, bond dimension recorded.

\textbf{Tier S, in four steps.} (1) \emph{Register.} On every instance
the realised register's own argmin is the model optimum (device rank 1);
full-matrix fidelity 0.21--0.32 vs 0.94--0.95 for the generic embedder,
which put the optimum at rank 9 on S\_seed04. (2) \emph{Feasibility.}
0.95--1.00 of raw shots are capacity-feasible (generic register:
0.06--0.22; uniform random: 0.02--0.05). (3) \emph{P(reach) vs controls
--- useful quantum work (brief §9.2).} The model-optimal clustering is
returned in 89--99 \% of raw shots on all five instances. The fair
control, \texttt{random-onehot}, reaches it with P = 2/16 ≈ 0.12 on
every instance (two labelings of one optimal clustering among 16
assignments; feasible 25--63 \%); the headline rows give 0.94 / 0.95 /
0.89 on S\_seed00/01/04 at equal decoding --- a 7--8× factor, so the
register, not the decoder, does the work (uniform bitstrings: 0.7--1.2
\%; generic embedder: 3--13 \%). qubo-solver's own pipeline (BLaDE + its
drive shaper, 5940 ns): 0.18--0.79 on four instances, 27 \% model gap on
S\_seed04; its tabu search finds every model optimum (its default SA, a
single-start greedy descent at \(T_0=5\), does not; we report a
stated-parameter SA instead). (4) \emph{Decomposition.} Where P(reach
\(C^*\)) is low with a 0 \% model gap (S\_seed03/04: 0.01 / 0.02) the
loss is the decomposition gap (15.6 \% / 21.7 \%; M\_seed02: 6.9 \%):
the closeness objective's optimum is not the CVRP optimum --- the cost
of cluster-first (§1), not of the device.

\textbf{Ablation --- the exactly-one margin is physically load-bearing.}
\texttt{onehot-ub-tight} replaces \(A=\max_i\sum_j w_{ij}+1\) by the
tight bound \(A=R_w/K\,(1+1\%)\) (still sound, checker-verified), same
register construction, pulse tuned for the original \(A\): P(reach model
optimum) 0.94 → 0.03 on S\_seed00 (device ground state = third-best
clustering), 0.95 → 0.52 on S\_seed01, 0.89 → 0.94 on S\_seed04 (qutip;
in-house SV 0.03 / 0.56 / 0.96): a smaller \(A\) shrinks the single-flip
defect gaps the sweep must resolve and leaves less margin for the
embedding error. The headline stays \texttt{onehot-ub}.

\textbf{Tier M --- the constraints embed, the objective does not
(DERIVATIONS §6.7, Q-M2).} \emph{Before:} our first M rows had
\(\delta_f=125.66\) rad/µs \(=2.28\,U_\text{clique}\), so two
excitations per triangle were favoured and 0 of 500 shots were decodable
(kept as \texttt{\textbar{}pre-6.7} in results.csv) --- a wrong sweep
end point, not a feasibility limit of the encoding; a uniform random
sampler decodes 0 of 2000 shots on every M instance (table), so the
device's ≥ 98 \% one-hot shots are its own work. \emph{After} (same
registers, bounded \(\delta_f\), emu-mps \(\chi=32\), 500 shots; rows
``ours (emu-mps)''): decodable 0 → 0.994 / 0.984 / 0.996, feasible 0 →
0.044 / 0.388 / 0.446 on M\_seed00/01/02, best model gap 67.9 / 37.9 /
23.0 \%, CVRP gap 29.9 / 9.2 / 16.0 \%. The optimum's one-hot rank is
unchanged (10 474 / 12 966 / 4 192 of 59 049): the bound restores
decodability; the distance couplings the plane cannot realise set the
quality. qubo-solver's independent pipeline (BLaDE register,
\(\chi=32\)) lands in the same regime on M\_seed00/01 (feasible 0.016 /

\hypertarget{what-did-not-work}{%
\section{6. What did not work}\label{what-did-not-work}}

\texttt{docs/DID\_NOT\_WORK.md}, one sentence each: no PyPI from the
sandboxes; CPLEX not importable; a typed number caught by review;
qubo-solver's default SA; a blocking calibration job; a weight scan with
no trend (the embedder's local minimum moves P(reach) more than the
weights); a non-reproducible embedder (fixed by committing registers);
the tight-\(A\) ablation; an unbounded \(\delta_f\) that made the first
tier-M rows undecodable (§6.7); a \texttt{\textbar{}} in a run tag that
lost one Windows record; and the one-hot \emph{ordering} not embedding
for \(K=3\) --- the limit of this encoding on this device.
