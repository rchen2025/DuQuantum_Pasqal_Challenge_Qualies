\hypertarget{model}{%
\section{1. Model}\label{model}}

\textbf{What we chose, why, and what it costs.} Cluster-first /
route-second: the register decides \emph{which} customers share a
vehicle; each cluster is then routed exactly (Held--Karp,
\texttt{cvrp/common.py}). The full (customer, vehicle, position)
encoding needs \(\sim N^2K\) variables --- 32 / 300 / 900 on S / M / L
--- against 80 atoms (DERIVATIONS Q1); clustering needs \(NK\). The
price is a \emph{decomposition gap} (our objective's best clustering is
not always the CVRP optimum), reported in §5.

\textbf{Variables and objective (brief §2).} Customers \(i=1..N\) with
demands \(d_i\), \(K\) identical vehicles of capacity \(Q\), Euclidean
costs \(d_{ij}\). One-hot \(x_{ik}\in\{0,1\}\) (customer \(i\) on
vehicle \(k\)), \(n=NK\) atoms: \textbf{8 / 30 / 60} on S / M / L, no
slack. Objective: total pairwise distance inside each cluster,
\(f=\sum_k\sum_{i<j} d_{ij}x_{ik}x_{jk}\). It is additive over clusters,
so an exact set-partition DP (\texttt{cvrp/exact.py}) certifies the
\emph{model} optimum on every tier.

\textbf{Constraints → penalties (DERIVATIONS §4,
\texttt{docs/MODEL.md}).} Exactly-one {[}={]}: squared penalty
\(A\sum_i(1-\sum_k x_{ik})^2\) → \(h=-A\) on every variable, \(J=2A\) on
each customer's \(K\)-clique. Capacity {[}≤{]}: unbalanced penalization
\(\sum_k[-\lambda_1(Q-L_k)+\lambda_2(Q-L_k)^2]\) (Montañez-Barrera et
al.~2024), expanded. Its per-customer linear part \(c_i\sum_k x_{ik}\)
is a \emph{constant} on one-hot strings (the one-hot identity), so it is
dropped: \(\lambda_1\) disappears and what remains is
\(+2\lambda_2 d_id_j\) on same-vehicle pairs, a \textbf{soft
load-balance term} \(\lambda_2\sum_k L_k^2\). For \(K=2\) its optimum is
capacity-feasible whenever a feasible split exists; for \(K\ge3\) only
likely. We therefore score feasible decodes only and report the
feasibility rate (Pitfall 9.1).

\textbf{What the device sees.} At \(\Omega=0\) the final Hamiltonian is
a QUBO with one \emph{uniform} linear coefficient (\(\delta_f\)) and
\emph{positive} couplings (\(C_6/r^6\)) --- exactly the pattern above:
every \(J>0\), every \(h=-A\). Weights:
\(A=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}(d_{ij}+2\lambda_2 d_id_j)+1\) (provable: every
argmin is one-hot), \(\lambda_2=0.15\,R/(2Q)\) from a 40-seed
brute-force scan. Checker on tier S (\texttt{out/gate1/}): argmin
feasible and optimal on 5/5 shipped instances; \(A\) survives one
halving, fails at ¼; \(\lambda_2\) not binding there.

\hypertarget{embedding-derivations-5}{%
\section{3. Embedding (DERIVATIONS §5)}\label{embedding-derivations-5}}

\textbf{What we chose, why, and what it costs.} A generic least-squares
placement cannot realise the one-hot rook's graph, so we fit the
register on the one-hot subspace only (§5.7). This works for \(K=2\)
(tier S) and fails for \(K=3\) (tier M).

\textbf{Why the generic embedder fails.} The \(K\) copies of a customer
form a \(2A\)-clique and must sit 5 µm apart, so a neighbouring
customer's atom is at nearly the same distance from all \(K\) copies:
the ``zero'' cross-vehicle couplings come out as large as the wanted
ones. Measured: rel. Frobenius error 0.95 (\(\|U/\alpha-J\|_F/\|J\|_F\)
over off-diagonals, always reported with the worst-pair error) and, on
S\_seed04, an infeasible device ground state (model optimum at rank 9).

\textbf{Ask for less.} On one-hot strings each customer pair contributes
exactly one coupling, so only \emph{same-vehicle minus cross-vehicle}
must equal \(\alpha w_{ij}\) (identity 5.7b); cross couplings need only
be constant. Each customer becomes a rigid 5 µm \(K\)-gon (clique pinned
at the minimum separation, \(\alpha=C_6/5^6/2A\)); centres and
orientations are fitted (L-BFGS, 6 seeded starts) so that the device
energies of all one-hot strings match \(\alpha E_\text{QUBO}\), with
explicit single-flip defect gaps and a fitted \(\delta_f\) (a constant
on the subspace, so it only sets the defect gaps). Registers are cached
and committed (\texttt{embeddings/}). Per run we record full-matrix and
subspace fidelity and the \emph{realised-register check}: is the
device's own argmin feasible and the model optimum, and the smallest
defect gap. Tier S: 5/5 yes.

\textbf{Where it breaks (tier M, \(K=3\)).} Triangles 5 µm on a side
cannot be far from each other's neighbours: the subspace-fit residual is
38 (S: 0.06--0.32), defects fall below their parents and the model
optimum ranks \textasciitilde10 000th of 59 049 one-hot strings; opening
the triangles to 6--10 µm lowers the energy scale without fixing the
ordering (rank ≥ 1 279; DERIVATIONS §5.7, DID\_NOT\_WORK). This is the
brief's §4 warning in its sharpest form: a property of one-hot encodings
on a 2-D \(1/r^6\) register, not of the weights.

\hypertarget{pulse-derivations-6}{%
\section{4. Pulse (DERIVATIONS §6)}\label{pulse-derivations-6}}

\textbf{Chosen: \(T=5800\) ns (device maximum minus 200 ns),
\(\Omega=0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\), fitted \(\delta_f\)} (35--38 rad/µs on
S, inside ±125.66). Shape: three-point Ω ramp (15 \% / 70 \% / 15 \% of
\(T\)) and a linear δ sweep from
\(\delta_0=-\min(\max(|\delta_f|,2\Omega_{\max}),\delta_{\max})\) to
\(\delta_f\) while Ω is on its plateau; every sequence is built with
Pulser on \texttt{AnalogDevice} and validated
(\texttt{to\_abstract\_repr()}, limits read from the device object).
Why: a scan on three tier-S instances (Fig. 1 right, §6.6), scored by
P(reach), best gap and feasibility, never mean cost: longer is
monotonically better (2000 → 4000 → 5800 ns at every Ω);
\(0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\) beats full and half power at 4000 and 5800 ns;
\(\delta_f\times\{0.8,1,1.25\}\) peaks at the fitted value.
S\_seed02/03, not in the scan, reach the model optimum in 99 \% / 96 \%
of shots. Emulators: pulser-simulation (qutip) for every headline row;
emu-sv cross-check (S\_seed00: 0.96 vs 0.94); an in-house exact
state-vector integrator built from Pulser's own samples for the scans
(TV 0.05--0.07 from qutip at 500 shots = sampling noise); emu-mps for
M/L, bond dimension recorded.

\textbf{Reading, in four steps.} (1) \emph{Register.} On every tier-S
instance the realised register's own argmin is the model optimum (device
rank 1); full-matrix fidelity 0.21--0.32 vs 0.94--0.95 for the generic
embedder, which put the optimum at rank 9 on S\_seed04. (2)
\emph{Feasibility.} 0.95--1.00 of raw shots decode to a
capacity-feasible assignment (generic register: 0.06--0.22; uniform
random: 0.02--0.05). (3) \emph{P(reach) against the controls --- useful
quantum work (brief §9.2).} The model-optimal clustering is returned in
89--99 \% of raw shots on all five instances. The fair control,
\texttt{random-onehot}, reaches it with P = 2/16 ≈ 0.12 on every
instance (two labelings of one optimal clustering among 16 assignments;
capacity-feasible 25--63 \%); the headline rows give 0.94 / 0.95 / 0.89
on S\_seed00/01/04 at equal decoding --- a 7--8× factor, so the
register, not the decoder, does the work (uniform bitstrings: 0.7--1.2
\%; generic embedder: 3--13 \%). qubo-solver's own pipeline (BLaDE + its
drive shaper, 5940 ns): 0.18--0.79 on four instances and a 27 \% model
gap on S\_seed04; its tabu search finds every model optimum (its default
SA, a single-start greedy descent at \(T_0=5\), does not; we report a
stated-parameter SA instead). (4) \emph{Decomposition.} Where P(reach
\(C^*\)) is low with a 0 \% model gap (S\_seed03/04: 0.01 / 0.02) the
loss is the decomposition gap (15.6 \% / 21.7 \%; M\_seed02: 6.9 \%):
the closeness objective's optimum is not the CVRP optimum --- the cost
of cluster-first (§1), not of the device.

\textbf{Ablation --- the exactly-one margin is physically load-bearing.}
\texttt{onehot-ub-tight} replaces \(A=\max_i\sum_j w_{ij}+1\) by the
tight bound \(A=R_w/K\,(1+1\%)\) (still sound, checker-verified), same
register construction, pulse tuned for the original \(A\). P(reach model
optimum) goes 0.94 → 0.03 on S\_seed00 (device ground state = third-best
clustering), 0.95 → 0.52 on S\_seed01 and 0.89 → 0.94 on S\_seed04
(qutip; in-house SV 0.03 / 0.56 / 0.96): a smaller \(A\) shrinks the
single-flip defect gaps the sweep must resolve and leaves less margin
for the embedding error. The headline stays \texttt{onehot-ub}.

\textbf{Tiers M and L --- the measured limit of the encoding.} The
one-hot register does not embed for \(K=3\) (§3, DERIVATIONS Q-M1):
generic and polygon registers both put the model optimum at rank
\textasciitilde10 000 of 59 049 one-hot strings, and a uniform random
sampler decodes 0 of 2000 shots. The measured consequence (table rows
M\_seed00): on emu-mps at \(\chi=32\) \textbf{0 of 500 shots are
feasible}, model and CVRP gaps are undefined, and the realised register
puts the model optimum at rank 10 474; the uniform random sampler is
0/2000 on M\_seed00/02. qubo-solver's tabu search on the same QUBO finds
feasible clusterings 17.6 \% / 2.8 \% above \(C^*\) on M\_seed01/02
(model gap 65.4 \% / 10.2 \%): the QUBO is solvable classically; the
register is what fails. M\_seed01/02, L\_seed00--02 (\(\chi=32\)) and an
M \(\chi=64\) convergence run are queued; we expect feasibility ≈ 0 and
report what is measured. A \(K=2\) bisection does not help either
(\texttt{docs/GATE2\_OPTIONS.md} (f): model optimum at rank 192 594 / 12
217).

\hypertarget{what-did-not-work}{%
\section{6. What did not work}\label{what-did-not-work}}

\texttt{docs/DID\_NOT\_WORK.md}, one sentence each: no PyPI from the
sandboxes; CPLEX not importable; a typed number caught by review;
qubo-solver's default SA; a blocking calibration job; a 12-point weight
scan with no trend (the embedder's local minimum moves P(reach) more
than the weights do); a non-reproducible embedding optimiser (fixed by
committing registers); the tight-\(A\) ablation; and one-hot not
embedding for \(K=3\) --- the limit of this encoding here.
