\hypertarget{model}{%
\section{1. Model}\label{model}}

\textbf{Bottom line.} The register decides which customers share a
vehicle; each group is then routed exactly. One atom per (customer,
vehicle) pair: 8 / 30 / 60 atoms on tiers S / M / L. On tier S the
device returns the best grouping in 89--99 \% of shots. On M it returns
valid groupings but poor ones. On L it returns almost nothing valid. §5
has the numbers, §6 the reasons.

\textbf{Why cluster-first.} Encoding the full route (customer, vehicle,
position) needs about \(N^2K\) atoms: 32 / 300 / 900 for S / M / L,
against 80 available (DERIVATIONS Q1). Deciding only the grouping needs
\(NK\). The cost is a \emph{decomposition gap}: the best grouping under
our objective is not always the grouping of the shortest routes. It is 0
\% on 5 of 11 instances and up to 21.7 \% on the rest (§5, decomposition
table).

\textbf{Variables.} \(N\) customers with demands \(d_i\); \(K\)
identical vehicles of capacity \(Q\); \(d_{ij}\) the distance between
customers \(i\) and \(j\). The bit \(x_{ik}=1\) means customer \(i\)
rides on vehicle \(k\). A valid grouping has exactly one \(1\) in every
customer's row (``one-hot'').

\textbf{Objective.} We want groups whose members are close to each
other, so we add up the distances between every pair of customers that
share a vehicle: \begin{equation}
f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ \sum_{i<j} d_{ij}\,x_{ik}\,x_{jk}.
\end{equation} It costs us the decomposition gap above, and it is
additive over groups, which is what lets an exact dynamic programme
certify the model optimum on every tier (\texttt{cvrp/exact.py}).

\textbf{Constraint 1 --- every customer on exactly one vehicle.}
Enforced by a squared penalty with weight \(A\): \begin{equation}
A\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(1-\sum_{k=1}^{K}x_{ik}\Big)^2 .
\end{equation} Expanded, it puts the same linear term \(-A\) on every
atom and a coupling \(+2A\) between the \(K\) atoms of one customer (a
``clique''). It costs nothing in atoms; it costs energy scale: \(A\)
must exceed every other term (below), and it is the largest coupling the
register has to realise.

\textbf{Constraint 2 --- vehicle load \(L_k=\sum_i d_i x_{ik}\) at most
\(Q\).} We use unbalanced penalization (Montañez-Barrera et al.~2024),
\(\sum_k\big[-\lambda_1(Q-L_k)+\lambda_2(Q-L_k)^2\big]\), because it
needs no slack atoms. On one-hot strings its linear part is the same
constant for every valid grouping, so it drops out together with
\(\lambda_1\) (the ``one-hot identity'', DERIVATIONS §4); what survives
is a load-balance term \begin{equation}
\lambda_2\sum_{k=1}^{K} L_k^2 \;=\; \text{const}+2\lambda_2\sum_k\sum_{i<j} d_i d_j\,x_{ik}x_{jk}.
\end{equation} It costs us a guarantee: for \(K=2\) its minimum is
capacity-feasible whenever a feasible split exists; for \(K\ge3\) it is
not guaranteed. We therefore score feasible decodes only and report the
feasibility rate (brief Pitfall 9.1).

\textbf{The whole cost function} is Eq. (1) + Eq. (2) + Eq. (3). Every
coupling is positive (\(d_{ij}\ge0\), \(2A>0\), \(2\lambda_2 d_id_j>0\))
and every linear term is \(-A\): exactly the pattern an analog register
with repulsive \(C_6/r^6\) interactions and one global detuning can
produce. The pairs that interact are the \(N{\times}K\) rook's graph
(same customer, or same vehicle); the capacity term adds no new pairs.

\textbf{Weights.} \(A=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}(d_{ij}+2\lambda_2 d_id_j)+1\),
which provably makes every minimiser one-hot (DERIVATIONS §4).
\(\lambda_2=0.15\,R/(2Q)\) with \(R=\max_i\sum_j d_{ij}\), from a
40-seed brute-force scan. Brute force on the five S instances: the
minimiser is feasible and optimal on 5/5; \(A\) survives one halving and
fails at \(A/4\); \(\lambda_2\) is not binding on any S instance.

\hypertarget{embedding-derivations-5}{%
\section{3. Embedding (DERIVATIONS §5)}\label{embedding-derivations-5}}

\textbf{Bottom line.} A generic least-squares placement cannot realise
the rook's graph. We fit the register only on the one-hot strings
instead. On tier S (\(K=2\)) that reproduces the model exactly; on M
(\(K=3\)) it reproduces the exactly-one constraint but not the ordering
of groupings; on L (\(K=4\)) the geometry of a square caps the energy
scale below what the sweep can resolve.

\textbf{Why generic placement fails.} The \(K\) atoms of one customer
must sit 5 µm apart (the clique). Any atom of another customer is then
at nearly the same distance from all \(K\) of them, so the couplings
that should be zero (different customer \emph{and} different vehicle)
come out as large as the wanted ones. Measured: relative coupling error
0.95, defined as \(\|U/\alpha-J\|_F/\|J\|_F\) over all pairs (lower is
better; \texttt{cvrp/embed.py}), and on S\_seed04 the register's ground
state was an infeasible string.

\textbf{Ask for less.} On a one-hot string each pair of customers has
exactly one active coupling, so only the difference \emph{same-vehicle
minus cross-vehicle} has to match the model (DERIVATIONS 5.7b); the
cross couplings only need to be constant. Each customer becomes a rigid
5 µm \(K\)-gon, which realises the clique exactly with
\(\alpha=C_6/(5\,\mu\text{m})^6/2A\); the centres and orientations are
fitted (L-BFGS, 6 seeded starts) so that the device energies of all
one-hot strings match \(\alpha\,E_\text{QUBO}\), with single-flip
defects forced above their parents and the final detuning \(\delta_f\)
as a fit parameter. Registers are committed in \texttt{embeddings/};
every run checks the realised register (is its own minimiser feasible
and optimal? smallest defect gap). Tier S: 5/5 yes, coupling error
0.21--0.32.

\textbf{A bound we found on tier M.} At the end of the sweep
(\(\Omega=0\)) one atom of a \(K\)-gon excited costs \(-\delta_f\); two
excited cost \(-2\delta_f+U_{\min}\), where \(U_{\min}\) is the coupling
of the \emph{weakest} pair inside the polygon. One excitation per
customer is the ground state only if \begin{equation}
0<\delta_f<U_{\min}.
\end{equation} For \(K\le3\) every pair is the 5 µm side,
\(U_{\min}=55.1\) rad/µs; the S fits land at \(\delta_f=35\)--38 rad/µs
(0.64--0.68 \(U_{\min}\)). On M the fit is ill-conditioned and ran to
the device maximum, 125.66 rad/µs \(=2.28\,U_{\min}\), which favours
\emph{two} vehicles per customer; the embedder now enforces Eq. (4)
(fallback \(0.65\,U_{\min}\)). For \(K=4\) a 5 µm square has 7.07 µm
diagonals, and \(1/r^6\) turns that \(\sqrt2\) into a factor 8:
\(U_{\min}=6.9\) rad/µs, so the L registers run at \(\delta_f=4.5\)
rad/µs. Four points in a plane are never equidistant, so a planar
one-hot clique for \(K\ge4\) is never uniform (DERIVATIONS §6.7).

\hypertarget{pulse-derivations-6}{%
\section{4. Pulse (DERIVATIONS §6)}\label{pulse-derivations-6}}

\textbf{Chosen: 5800 ns (device maximum minus 200 ns),
\(\Omega=0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\), fitted \(\delta_f\).} Three-point
\(\Omega\) ramp (15 \% up, 70 \% plateau, 15 \% down) and a linear
detuning sweep during the plateau from
\(\delta_0=-\min(\max(|\delta_f|,2\Omega_{\max}),\delta_{\max})\) to
\(\delta_f\). Starting far below resonance puts every atom in
\(|g\rangle\); ending at \(\delta_f>0\) with \(\delta_f<U_{\min}\) makes
one excitation per customer the target state. Every sequence is built
with Pulser on \texttt{AnalogDevice} and validated against the device
object.

\textbf{Why these values.} A scan on three S instances (Fig. 1 right;
DERIVATIONS §6.6), scored by P(reach), best gap and feasibility, never
mean cost: longer is better at every \(\Omega\) (2000 → 4000 → 5800 ns);
\(0.75\,\Omega_{\max}\) beats full and half power at 4000 and 5800 ns;
\(\delta_f\times\{0.8,1,1.25\}\) gives 0.915 / 0.932 / 0.884, so the
fitted value stays. S\_seed02/03, not used in the scan, reach the model
optimum in 99 \% / 96 \% of shots with these settings. Emulators:
pulser-simulation (QuTiP) for every S headline row; emu-sv cross-check
on S\_seed00 (0.96 vs 0.94); an in-house exact state-vector integrator
built from Pulser's own samples for the scans (total-variation distance
0.05--0.07 from QuTiP at 500 shots, i.e.~sampling noise); emu-mps for M
and L with the bond dimension recorded.

\textbf{Tier S --- the device does the work.} The model-optimal grouping
is returned in 89--99 \% of raw shots on all five instances, with
0.95--1.00 of shots feasible. The fair control --- random
assignment-valid strings through the same decoder --- reaches it with
probability 2/16 ≈ 0.12 on every instance; uniform random bitstrings
0.7--1.2 \%; the generic embedder 3--13 \%. qubo-solver's own pipeline
(its embedding and drive, 5940 ns) reaches 0.78 / 0.78 / 0.18 / 0.79 /
0.00 and has a 27 \% model gap on S\_seed04; its tabu search finds every
model optimum classically. Where P(reach \(C^*\)) is low with a 0 \%
model gap (S\_seed03/04: 0.01 / 0.02) the loss is the decomposition gap
(15.6 \% / 21.7 \%): the closeness objective's best grouping is not the
shortest-route grouping. That is the price of §1, not of the device.

\textbf{Ablation --- the margin in \(A\) is physical.} Replacing \(A\)
by its tight provable bound (DERIVATIONS Q-A), same register
construction and pulse, moves P(reach model optimum) from 0.94 to 0.03
on S\_seed00, 0.95 to 0.52 on S\_seed01 and 0.89 to 0.94 on S\_seed04: a
smaller \(A\) shrinks the defect gaps the sweep must resolve.

\textbf{Tier M --- valid groupings, poor ones.} With \(\delta_f\)
bounded by Eq. (4), 98--100 \% of shots are one-hot and 4.4 \% / 38.8 \%
/ 44.6 \% are capacity-feasible on M\_seed00/01/02; the best feasible
shots are 67.9 / 37.9 / 23.0 \% above the model optimum (29.9 / 9.2 /
16.0 \% CVRP gap). A uniform random sampler decodes 0 of 2000 shots on
every M instance. We enumerated all 59 049 one-hot groupings to locate
the loss (DERIVATIONS Q-M3, \texttt{docs/checks/landscapes.md}): (i) the
load-balance term leaves the model optimum at QUBO rank 1 on
M\_seed00/01, but on M\_seed02 the QUBO minimum is a feasible grouping
10.2 \% above it --- exactly what qubo-solver's tabu search returns ---
so on M\_seed02 about 10 of the 23 points are the \(\lambda_2\) rule;
(ii) under the realised register the model optimum sits at rank 10 474 /
12 966 / 4 192 of 59 049, which is the main loss; (iii) the emulated
best shots beat the register's own best feasible string (82.0 / 101.3 /
93.9 \%), so the sweep is not the bottleneck. qubo-solver's independent
pipeline lands in the same regime (feasible 0.016 / 0.37 / 0.388, CVRP
gap 35.2 / 11.2 / 13.6 \%), while its tabu search on the same QUBO
reaches 17.6 \% / 2.8 \% above \(C^*\) on M\_seed01/02: the QUBO is
solvable classically; the planar register limits it. Emulator check:
M\_seed00 needs a bond dimension of only 12, so \(\chi=32\) and
\(\chi=64\) give the same untruncated run (feasible 0.044 vs 0.030, gap
67.9 vs 70.2 \%: sampling noise); M\_seed01 and L hit the \(\chi=32\)
cap and are provisional (DERIVATIONS §6.8).

\textbf{Tier L --- almost nothing valid.} L\_seed00 (60 atoms, emu-mps
\(\chi=32\), 500 shots, \(\delta_f=4.475\) rad/µs): 1.4 \% of shots
one-hot, 0.8 \% feasible, best feasible shot 140 \% above the model
optimum (79.7 \% CVRP gap). The cause is Eq. (4): with \(U_{\min}=6.9\)
rad/µs the whole one-hot landscape of 60 atoms is compressed into a few
rad/µs (smallest defect gap 1.1 rad/µs against 19--23 on M), and a sweep
of 30 rad/µs in 4 µs is far from adiabatic there (rate ≈ 7 rad/µs²
against a gap-squared of ≈ 1.2). The model optimum sits at one-hot rank
16 534 / 1 063 / 14 862 of 1 048 576 on L\_seed00/01/02; L\_seed01/02
rows and an M\_seed00 \(\chi=64\) convergence run are in
\texttt{results.csv} as measured.

\hypertarget{what-did-not-work}{%
\section{6. What did not work}\label{what-did-not-work}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Generic embedding of the one-hot graph.} Tried least-squares
  placement of all \(NK\) atoms; coupling error 0.95, an infeasible
  ground state on S\_seed04, P(reach) 3--13 \%. Cause: the clique forces
  the zero couplings to be as large as the wanted ones. Replaced by the
  one-hot-subspace fit (§3): P(reach) 0.89--0.99.
\item
  \textbf{Unbounded \(\delta_f\) on tier M.} The first M rows had
  \(\delta_f=125.66\) rad/µs \(=2.28\,U_{\min}\): 0 of 500 shots
  decodable on M\_seed00/02. Cause: two excitations per triangle were
  favoured. Fixed by Eq. (4); the old rows stay in \texttt{results.csv}
  under \texttt{\textbar{}pre-6.7}. Then the triangle version of the
  bound was applied to tier-L squares: the first L run
  (\(\delta_f=38.7\)) was killed before reporting and the bound
  rewritten for the weakest pair.
\item
  \textbf{The one-hot ordering for \(K=3\) and \(K=4\).} Opening the M
  triangles from 5 to 6--10 µm lowers the energy scale without fixing
  the ordering (optimum rank ≥ 1 279); a \(K=2\) bisection puts it at
  rank 192 594 / 12 217 (\texttt{docs/GATE2\_OPTIONS.md}). We did not
  find a planar register that orders one-hot groupings by Eq. (1) for
  \(K\ge3\).
\item
  \textbf{The \(\lambda_2\) rule on M\_seed02.} The load-balance weight
  moves the QUBO optimum 10.2 \% above the model optimum on one of three
  M instances (0 \% elsewhere). We kept the rule because changing
  weights after seeing results was ruled out; the effect is quantified
  in Q-M3.
\item
  \textbf{Register reproducibility.} The polygon optimiser gave two
  different 30-atom registers from one seed when two runs overlapped
  (M\_seed01, optimum rank 1 114 vs 12 966). Rule adopted: every
  reported row loads the committed register; one control row was
  re-measured.
\item
  \textbf{Classical references.} CPLEX is not importable; the S
  agreement check is brute force vs two annealers. qubo-solver's default
  annealer is a single-start descent at \(T_0=5\) and misses the S
  optima, so a stated-parameter annealer is used.
\item
  \textbf{Weight scan.} A 12-point scan of \((A,\lambda_2)\) around the
  rule showed no trend: the embedder's local minimum moves P(reach) more
  than the weights do (DERIVATIONS §4.1).
\item
  \textbf{Tooling.} A \texttt{\textbar{}} in a run tag lost one Windows
  record; a silent LaTeX failure left a stale PDF once. Both are caught
  by the build scripts now.
\end{itemize}
